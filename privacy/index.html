privacy notice

Last updated 12 November 2026. Published at skriblio.com/privacy.

Skriblio is made by STEADY LABS (PTY) LTD, registration 2026/086606/07 ("we"). Questions and requests, including anything for our Information Officer, go to info@skriblio.com and a person answers.

The short version. Your journal is encrypted on its way to our servers and while it's stored there. We never sell it or use it for advertising, and it is never used to train AI. A friend only sees a page you choose to send them. You can lock your memories with Face ID or a fingerprint, export everything at any time, and deleting your account deletes your journal.

This notice says what we collect, why, where it goes, how long we keep it, and what you can do about it. It is written to match what the app actually does. When the app changes, this changes with it, and the date at the top moves.

Who the app is for

Age. You must be 16 or older to create an account. If we learn that someone under 16 has an account, we delete it. People aged 16 and 17 get the same defaults as everyone: their pages are private, there are no advertisements, and nothing about them is profiled or used to choose what they see.

What we collect and why

Your account. Your email address, to sign you in with a one time code, Sign in with Apple, or Google, and to reach you about your account. We do not store a password. Apple may give us a private relay address instead of your real one. Google gives us the address and the name on that Google account, and we keep both with your sign in.

Your journal. The text you write, photographs you attach, drawings you make, voice notes you record, the date and time of each entry, an optional rating, an optional title, and which of them you added to your favourites. This is the product. We store it so it is there when you open skriblio again and so you can search it. When you search your journal, the words you search for go to our server to find the pages; we don't keep them, apart from the server's own request logs, which are deleted within 7 days. If you connect a computer, a page you type there is part of the same journal, and we store the computer's name so you can tell your connected computers apart. An unsaved draft stays on that computer until you save or throw it away. What a connected computer can read from your journal is under "Your journal on a connected computer" below.

A place on an entry. From version 1.2.5. Skriblio asks for your location only when you tap "add location" on an entry, only while the app is open, and never in the background. Each tap is a new yes: nothing is added on its own. Your phone's own place lookup, Apple's on an iPhone and Google's on Android, turns your exact position into the name of a neighbourhood or town. The exact position goes only to that service, from your phone, once for each tap. Skriblio never receives it. What we keep with the entry is that name and the position rounded to about a kilometre, never a street or an address. If your phone has no place lookup or it gives no answer, the entry keeps the rounded position with no name, and the phone may later send that rounded position, not the exact one, to the same service to try again for the name. While you are offline, or before the entry has reached us, the place waits on your phone for up to 14 days. The place shows on that entry and on the map in memories. It does not go to the desktop companion, a widget, a friend or analytics, and it is not part of a page you send to a friend. Tap the place on the entry to remove it, and it is deleted. It is also deleted when the entry is deleted for good, and when you delete your account. Your export includes it. On Android the app can only ask for an approximate location.

A letter to yourself. From version 1.2.5. A letter you write to open on a later day is stored on our servers like any other entry, and the same rules about who may read an entry apply to it. Until the day and time you chose, the app shows none of it: not in memories, search, widgets or on a connected computer, and your export lists only the day it will open, without its words. Nobody can open a letter early, including you. On the day, your phone shows you one notification, made by the phone itself and holding no words from the letter, and the letter becomes an ordinary entry. We keep the day it opens with the entry. The exact time you chose is kept only on the phone that sealed it, so another phone of yours opens it at 09:00 your time on that day. A connected computer may keep a letter hidden until the end of its opening day. You can delete a waiting letter without opening it, and it is deleted for good, not moved to recently deleted. Deleting your account deletes it.

Importing a journal. From version 1.2.5. You can bring in a journal file you exported from another app. The phone opens and reads the file itself: the file is not uploaded. A copy the phone makes of it to read is removed when the import ends. What is uploaded is the result: the entries, with their text, titles, dates and stars, and their photographs, saved and stored like any entry you write. Anything else in the file, such as places, weather, tags and moods, is dropped on the phone and never reaches us. Photographs from an import are not sent to Anthropic or read by any model, and an import sends nothing to your friends. Analytics counts that an import finished and a rough size band for its number of entries, never what was in it.

Feedback you send. From version 1.2.5. If you tap "send feedback" in settings, we receive the words you type, the optional line about what you were doing, the version of the app, whether your phone is an iPhone or an Android, and your account. It is tied to your account, it is never an entry, and it is never shown to anyone else. Only skriblio staff can read it, and the app cannot read it back, so it is not in your export. We use it to answer you and to fix things. We keep it until your account is deleted, which deletes it too. We set no earlier date for removing it; to have a message removed sooner, write to info@skriblio.com. Analytics counts that feedback was sent, never what it said.

Handwriting turned into text. When you attach a photograph of a handwritten page and you have said yes to "turn handwriting into text", we send the image to a vision model at Anthropic, which turns the handwriting into text so you can search it. The app asks once, after your first photograph of a page. If you haven't said yes, your phone reads the page where it can and the image is not sent. On Android your phone reads a page with Google's ML Kit text recognition, which is part of the app and runs on the phone: the picture and the words it reads are not sent to Google or to us for that. ML Kit does send Google usage metrics, such as the kind of device, the app, how long a read took, the size of the picture and error codes, and there is no setting to switch that off. The text is stored with the entry. Anthropic processes the image to return the text and, under our agreement with them, does not use it to train models. If you correct a word, that swap stays on this phone and is used only to fix later text made from handwriting on that phone. It is not sent to us or to a model. You can delete the text, or delete the photograph, at any time. Your yes belongs to your account, and you can take it back with the switch "turn handwriting into text" in settings: from then on no photograph is sent, and text already made stays with its entry until you remove it there.

A copy on your phone. So your memories open without a signal, the app keeps a copy of your recent entries and your memory tree on your phone, inside the app's own storage, which the phone encrypts while it is locked. It is replaced each time your memories load and removed when you sign out. On Android the app leaves its storage out of Google's automatic backup and phone to phone transfer, so this copy, entries that have not reached us yet and a draft never go into a backup. A new or restored Android phone gets your journal back from your account when you sign in.

Voice notes. A voice note is stored exactly as you recorded it, with your journal. If you ask for one to be typed up, your phone does it with its own speech recognition, and the recording is not sent to us or to anyone else for that. On Android 13 and later the app asks the phone's recognizer to work on the phone only, and only offers to type a voice note up when the phone has that language installed; Android installs the language itself, and the app sends no audio anywhere for it. So that typing up works without a connection, the app also keeps a copy of each recording you make on this phone, inside the app. That copy is removed when you delete the voice note, when its entry is deleted for good, and when you sign out or delete your account. The typed words are stored with the entry so you can search them. You can remove the typed words, or delete the voice note, at any time.

Your banana. Which character you picked, the name you gave it, and the date of your last entry. This drives what the banana says and when. The kind of entry you saved each day (typed, photograph, voice note or drawing) is kept on your phone only, so your banana's world can answer it. It is not sent to us, and never to analytics.

Widgets. The home screen widgets show your banana, a prompt, the days you journaled this week and, from your favourites, a page or a photo from your memory tree. To draw them, the app keeps that small amount of information in storage on your phone that only the app and its widgets can read.

The desktop companion. To walk around your screen and keep out of the way during calls, the companion looks, on your computer, at where other windows are, which app each one belongs to (never their titles or what is in them), and whether the camera or microphone is in use. To open its panel when you push the pointer against the edge of the screen, it also looks at where the pointer is and whether a mouse button is down. None of that leaves your computer. The panel you journal in is left out of screen shares, recordings and screenshots where your operating system supports that. The companion updates itself. When it starts, and then about once a day, it asks skriblio.com whether a newer version exists. The request carries only the companion's own version, your operating system and its processor type, and nothing about you or your journal. A newer version is downloaded in the background, its signature is checked against our public key (a package that fails the check is thrown away), and it is put in the next time the companion starts. Older builds that cannot update themselves ask for a version number twice a day and offer a download link.

Your journal on a connected computer. From phone version 1.2.5. A computer you have connected can show your memory tree, a list of your entries, search, and one entry at a time with its photographs and voice notes. From the computer you can also add an entry to your favourites or take it out. A computer can do this only while it is connected, and disconnecting it on your phone ends it. Before the companion shows any of it, it asks your computer to check that it is you: Touch ID or your password on a Mac, Windows Hello or your password on Windows. The check is done by your operating system on your computer, and your fingerprint, your face and your password never reach us. The companion asks again after a period of use and after the computer has slept or been locked. Everything it shows comes through our server, which answers only for your own account. Entries, photographs and voice notes you look at are fetched when you open them and are not saved on the computer, and photographs and voice notes reach it through links that our server signs for files in your own folder only. Each link stops working after one hour. When you search from a connected computer, the words you search for go to our server to find the pages, and we don't keep them, apart from the server's own request logs, which are deleted within 7 days.

On your phone you can hide an entry from memories or blur its words. So that a connected computer never shows those entries, your phone sends us the list of which entries they are, and our server leaves them out of everything it gives a computer. We store that list with the name of the phone that sent it, and the time it was last sent with your account. Until your phone has sent it once, which needs version 1.2.5, a computer is shown nothing from your journal. Deleted entries, and letters to yourself that are still waiting, are never shown on a computer either, and neither is the place on an entry. You can clear a list left by a phone you no longer use from "hidden entries" on your phone. The list goes when you un-hide the entry, when the entry is deleted for good, and when you delete your account.

Subscription. Purchases go through Apple or Google. We never see your card. RevenueCat, our subscription service, tells us whether your subscription is active and until when, and we store that date. RevenueCat receives your account id and an identifier for your device, so a purchase can be matched to your account.

Device and usage. Product analytics through PostHog and Google Analytics: which screens you open, how you opened the app (from the icon, a widget or a reminder) and how smoothly the worlds draw (a bucket of slow frames in one minute), which buttons you tap, which prompt you used (prompts about hard days and the deeper prompts of the day are counted only as "other"), that you journaled on a day, which first week step you finished and how many days in, whether a purchase was the free weeks, the country of your App Store or Google Play account when the plans page is shown (two letters, read from the store, not from where you are), before you have a plan that the plans page opened and which of a few fixed actions opened it (for example "journal" or "appearance"), that you sent feedback or finished an import (never what was in it), and which of our reminder and friend screens you saw and what you answered. Events name the screens and buttons you use, so one can show that the voice recorder or the camera was opened. No event carries your words, your photographs, what a voice note says, how long an entry was, how you rated the day or a place you add to an entry. "Share usage numbers" in settings turns all of this off on that phone. PostHog links these events to your account id, and its location lookup is switched off. Google Analytics uses a random id instead of your account, and works out an approximate location, such as the city, from your connection without keeping the IP address. That is separate from a place you add to an entry, which analytics never includes. Each of these services keeps a random id for your install. Crash reports through Sentry when the app fails, scrubbed on the phone of journal text and email, and stored without your IP address. The app sends no push notifications from a server and stores no push token. Our own ads. We advertise skriblio on Instagram, and to see which ads work, the iPhone and Android apps include Meta's software development kit. It tells Meta that the app was installed and opened, with an anonymous id for your install, and RevenueCat tells Meta when a free trial starts or a plan is paid for, with that same anonymous id. Meta uses this to count results for our ads. The app never asks to track you and never reads your advertising identifier: on iPhone Meta only gets what Apple allows without that permission, and on Android the app does not hold the advertising id permission at all. Nothing you journal, no photographs, no voice notes and not your email ever go to Meta. We don't show ads in the app and we never sell your data.

Reminders. Reminders are optional and off unless you choose them. The app asks once during your first run, and again after a save if you have not answered; either way, nothing is set until you pick how often, and "not now" sets nothing. They are made on your phone by the phone itself, on a schedule it keeps, so nothing is sent to a server and no push token is stored. A reminder holds a line from the banana or a prompt to try, and never anything from your journal. If you choose the time it learns, the app keeps the time of day of your recent saves, on your phone only, to find the hour you usually journal. Choosing no reminders in the app clears the list. Notifications need your phone's permission, which you can withdraw in its settings at any time.

Friends. If you journal with friends, we keep your own name if you set one, the first name you typed for each friend, your banana, who you journal with, a hashed copy of each open invite (never the link) and when each was made. Each invite also has a friend code, four letters taken from the name you typed and three random digits, such as JACO 482. The current app only uses the link, but we still make a code so a friend on an older version of the app can type it instead, and it is removed when the invite is used, cancelled or expires. A code is a small secret, so we count wrong codes (the person who typed them, and when) for two days at most and stop a person after about ten wrong ones in an hour. A friend who opens your link, or types a code, sees your banana and the name you typed on the invite before they say yes, and nothing else; the server answers with those two things and no others. Your friends' apps can learn if you've journaled today, worked out from your pages' dates in your own time zone, and, only when you have both been away a while, that you both have. They see your banana and your own name. We never share a page, a title, a photo, a time or how many days. All of it is deleted when journaling together ends (an ended one is kept until you've both seen it, thirty days at most) or when you delete your account. Pages sent to a friend. If you choose to send a page to a friend you journal with, we make one copy of that page for them: its title and words, its doodle, its first photo and its first voice note if it has them, the short line you add, and the first name you typed for yourself in that friendship. The copy is stored in a private folder that only your friend can read, and it has no link back to your page, so editing or deleting your page changes nothing on their side. Your friend sees that one page and nothing else from your journal. We keep when it was sent, when your banana delivered it, when your friend took it off their tree and, if they reported it, when and why. No notification is sent: your friend finds out the next time they open skriblio. When your friend takes a page off their tree, we delete the copy at once. If they report it, we keep the copy so it can be reviewed, and delete it once the report is dealt with. A friend can block you, and then nothing more reaches them from you. You can send each friend one page a week. A page you sent is deleted when you delete your account or when your friend deletes theirs.

Journal school and your first week. Journal school progress and your first week (six steps: write, pick a world, start from a prompt, try a way to journal, record a voice note, scan a page or take a photo), each with the day it was first done, are kept on your account so a new phone has them. They hold step names and dates only, never a prompt or what you wrote. They are in your export and deleted with your account.

Care log. A short record of what the banana did and when, such as the day you came back after a quiet spell. It is included when you export your journal.

Lawful basis (for readers in the EU and UK)

Performance of a contract for the journal, account and subscription features you asked for. Consent for turning the handwriting in your photographs into text in the cloud (the app asks once, before any photograph leaves the phone for a model, and you can take it back in settings). Consent for a place on an entry, given by tapping "add location" each time and your phone's own permission. Legitimate interest for crash reporting and product analytics. You can switch product analytics off on a phone in settings, under "share usage numbers", and from then on that phone sends nothing to PostHog or Google Analytics and no automatic events to Meta. You can object to crash reporting by emailing us.

Where your data lives

Our database and file storage are with Supabase in Frankfurt, Germany. Images sent to have their handwriting turned into text go to Anthropic in the United States, which returns the text, does not train on them, and keeps inputs only for a limited time for safety and abuse checks. The exact position behind "add location" goes from your phone to Apple's place lookup on an iPhone or Google's on Android, never through us. Sign in emails are sent through Resend. RevenueCat, PostHog, Google Analytics, Sentry and Meta are in the United States or the EU depending on region; see the processor list at skriblio.com/privacy/processors. Transfers out of South Africa and the EU rest on those providers' standard contractual clauses and data processing agreements.

Who sees your data

Us, to run the service and to help you when you write in. The processors above, each only for their one job. Nobody else, unless the law compels us, in which case we tell you if we are allowed to.

How long we keep it

Your journal, for as long as your account exists. Deleted entries are removed from your view immediately, wait 30 days in recently deleted, and are then purged from our database and storage. A place on an entry, until you remove it or the entry is purged. A letter to yourself, until you delete it or, once it has opened, as any entry. A list of entries a connected computer leaves out, until you un-hide the entry, the entry is purged or your account is deleted. Feedback you send, until your account is deleted. Friends: an open invite and its friend code until it is used, cancelled or seven days pass; wrong friend codes, two days; a friendship until either of you ends it, then until you've both seen that, thirty days at most. Pages sent to a friend: until your friend takes them off their tree, a reported page until its report is dealt with, and every one of them when either of you deletes an account. The record that one was sent, for the weekly limit, stays for as long as both accounts exist. Journal school and first week records, for as long as your account exists. Server request logs, 7 days. A downloaded companion update, until it is put in or replaced. Analytics events, 12 months. Crash reports, 90 days. When you delete your account, everything we hold is deleted, your stored files first (photographs, voice notes and drawings), then the account, which removes every row that belongs to it. On the phone you delete it from, the app also removes entries that had not reached us yet, their photographs and voice notes, and any draft. Backups roll off within 30 days. Our processors delete what they hold on their own schedules: analytics events within 12 months, crash reports within 90 days, and the stores and RevenueCat keep purchase records as the law and their rules require.

Your rights

You can export everything from inside the app ('you', then 'settings', then 'export data'), with or without a subscription; without one, 'export data' is on the plans page. You can delete your account from inside the app, and it is immediate. If you no longer have the app, write to info@skriblio.com from the address you sign in with and we delete it within 30 days; skriblio.com/delete-account says how, and how to delete only some of it. You can ask us to correct something, to stop analytics for your account, or to tell you exactly what we hold, by emailing info@skriblio.com. We answer within 30 days. If you are unhappy with our answer you may complain to the Information Regulator of South Africa or, in the EU and UK, to your local supervisory authority.

Security

Your journal is encrypted in transit and at rest. It is not end to end encrypted, because searching your pages and turning your handwriting into text happen on our server. Access is limited to the service itself, and to the director of the company only for support you ask for. You can put your memories and your entries behind Face ID, Touch ID, or your device's equivalent; home and your banana stay open.

Children

The service is for people aged 16 and over. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 16.

Changes

We post changes here with a new date and, for anything that changes what we collect or why, we tell you in the app before it applies.
