privacy notice

Last updated 8 October 2026. Published at skriblio.com/privacy.

Skriblio is made by STEADY LABS (PTY) LTD, registration 2026/086606/07 ("we"). Questions and requests, including anything for our Information Officer, go to info@skriblio.com and a person answers.

This notice says what we collect, why, where it goes, how long we keep it, and what you can do about it. It is written to match what the app actually does. When the app changes, this changes with it, and the date at the top moves.

Who the app is for

Adults. You must be 18 or older to create an account. If we learn that someone under 18 has an account, we delete it.

What we collect and why

Your account. Your email address, to sign you in with a one time code, Sign in with Apple, or Google, and to reach you about your account. We do not store a password. Apple may give us a private relay address instead of your real one. Google gives us the address and the name on that Google account, and we keep both with your sign in.

Your journal. The text you write, photographs you attach, drawings you make, voice notes you record, the date and time of each entry, an optional rating, an optional title, and which of them you added to your favourites. This is the product. We store it so it is there when you come back and so you can search it. When you search your journal, the words you search for go to our server to find the pages; we don't keep them, apart from the server's own request logs, which are deleted within 7 days. If you pair a computer, a page you type there is part of the same journal, and we store the computer's name so you can tell your paired computers apart. An unsaved draft stays on that computer until you save or throw it away. The computer never lists the vault.

Transcripts of your photographs. When you attach a photograph of a handwritten page and you have said yes on the card the app shows once, we send the image to a vision model at Anthropic to turn the handwriting into text, so you can search it. If you haven't said yes, your phone reads the page where it can and the image is not sent. The transcript is stored with the entry. Anthropic processes the image to return the text and, under our agreement with them, does not use it to train models. If you correct a word, that swap stays on this phone and is used only to fix later transcripts on that phone. It is not sent to us or to a model. You can delete a transcript, or delete the photograph, at any time.

A copy on your phone. So the vault opens without a signal, the app keeps a copy of your recent entries and your memory tree on your phone, inside the app's own storage, which the phone encrypts while it is locked. It is replaced each time the vault loads and removed when you sign out.

Voice notes. A voice note is stored exactly as you recorded it, with your journal. If you ask for one to be typed up, your phone does it with its own speech recognition, and the recording is not sent to us or to anyone else for that. The typed words are stored with the entry so you can search them. You can remove the typed words, or delete the voice note, at any time.

Your banana. Which character you picked, the name you gave it, and the date of your last entry. This drives what the banana says and when.

Widgets. The home screen widgets show your banana, a prompt, the days you journaled this week and, from your favourites, a page or a photo from your memory tree. To draw them, the app keeps that small amount of information in storage on your phone that only the app and its widgets can read.

The desktop companion. To walk around your screen and keep out of the way during calls, the companion looks, on your computer, at where other windows are, which app each one belongs to (never their titles or what is in them), and whether the camera or microphone is in use. None of that leaves your computer. The companion updates itself. When it starts, and then about once a day, it asks skriblio.com whether a newer version exists. The request carries only the companion's own version, your operating system and its processor type, and nothing about you or your journal. A newer version is downloaded in the background, its signature is checked against our public key (a package that fails the check is thrown away), and it is put in the next time the companion starts. Older builds that cannot update themselves ask for a version number twice a day and offer a download link.

Subscription. Purchases go through Apple or Google. We never see your card. RevenueCat, our subscription service, tells us whether your subscription is active and until when, and we store that date. RevenueCat receives your account id and an identifier for your device, so a purchase can be matched to your account.

Device and usage. Product analytics through PostHog and Google Analytics: which screens you open, which buttons you tap, which prompt you used, how you rated a day and how long a voice note was, so we can see what is confusing. Never your words, your photographs or what a voice note says. PostHog links these events to your account id, and its location lookup is switched off. Google Analytics uses a random id instead of your account, and works out an approximate location, such as the city, from your connection without keeping the IP address. Each of these services keeps a random id for your install. Crash reports through Sentry when the app fails, scrubbed on the phone of journal text and email, and stored without your IP address. The app does not send push notifications at the moment, so no push token is stored. No advertising identifiers, no advertising networks, no selling or sharing of data for advertising, ever.

Friends. If you journal with friends, we keep the first name you typed for each friend, your banana, who you journal with, a hashed copy of each open invite (never the link) and when each was made. Each invite also has a friend code, four letters taken from the name you typed and three random digits, such as JACO 482. We keep the code with the invite so a friend can type it instead of tapping the link, and it is removed when the invite is used, cancelled or expires. A code is a small secret, so we count wrong codes (the person who typed them, and when) for two days at most and stop a person after about ten wrong ones in an hour. The friend who types a code sees your banana and the name you typed before they say yes, and nothing else. Your friends' apps can learn if you've journaled today, worked out from your pages' dates in your own time zone, and, only when you have both been away a while, that you both have. They see your banana and the name you typed. We never share a page, a title, a photo, a time or how many days. All of it is deleted when journaling together ends (an ended one is kept until you've both seen it, thirty days at most) or when you delete your account.

Care log. A short record of what the banana did and when, such as the day you came back after a quiet spell. It is included when you export your journal.

Lawful basis (for readers in the EU and UK)

Performance of a contract for the journal, account and subscription features you asked for. Consent for cloud transcription (a card asks once, before any photograph leaves the phone for a model). Legitimate interest for crash reporting and product analytics, which you can object to by emailing us.

Where your data lives

Our database and file storage are with Supabase in Frankfurt, Germany. Images sent for transcription go to Anthropic in the United States, which returns the text, does not train on them, and keeps inputs only for a limited time for safety and abuse checks. Sign in emails are sent through Resend. RevenueCat, PostHog, Google Analytics and Sentry are in the United States or the EU depending on region; see the processor list at skriblio.com/privacy/processors. Transfers out of South Africa and the EU rest on those providers' standard contractual clauses and data processing agreements.

Who sees your data

Us, to run the service and to help you when you write in. The processors above, each only for their one job. Nobody else, unless the law compels us, in which case we tell you if we are allowed to.

How long we keep it

Your journal, for as long as your account exists. Deleted entries are removed from your view immediately, wait 30 days in recently deleted, and are then purged from our database and storage. Friends: an open invite and its friend code until it is used, cancelled or seven days pass; wrong friend codes, two days; a friendship until either of you ends it, then until you've both seen that, thirty days at most. Server request logs, 7 days. A downloaded companion update, until it is put in or replaced. Analytics events, 12 months. Crash reports, 90 days. When you delete your account, everything we hold is deleted, your stored files first (photographs, voice notes and drawings), then the account, which removes every row that belongs to it. Backups roll off within 30 days. Our processors delete what they hold on their own schedules: analytics events within 12 months, crash reports within 90 days, and the stores and RevenueCat keep purchase records as the law and their rules require.

Your rights

You can export everything from inside the app ('you', then 'export data'), with or without a subscription; without one, 'export data' is on the plans page. You can delete your account from inside the app, and it is immediate. If you no longer have the app, write to info@skriblio.com from the address you sign in with and we delete it within 30 days; skriblio.com/delete-account says how, and how to delete only some of it. You can ask us to correct something, to stop analytics for your account, or to tell you exactly what we hold, by emailing info@skriblio.com. We answer within 30 days. If you are unhappy with our answer you may complain to the Information Regulator of South Africa or, in the EU and UK, to your local supervisory authority.

Security

Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Your journal is not end to end encrypted: our servers can read it, because transcription and search need to. Access is limited to the service and to the director of the company for support you ask for. You can put your vault and your pages behind Face ID, Touch ID, or your device's equivalent; home and your banana stay open.

Children

The service is for adults. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18.

Changes

We post changes here with a new date and, for anything that changes what we collect or why, we tell you in the app before it applies.
