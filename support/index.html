support.

most of it's below. for anything else, write to info@skriblio.com, and a person answers, usually the same day.

how do i cancel

apple or google handle the subscription, so you cancel it with them. on an iphone, open settings, tap your name, then 'subscriptions' and skriblio. for android, open the play store, tap your profile, then 'payments and subscriptions'. you can also open 'subscription' in the 'you' tab and tap 'manage subscription'. after you cancel, you keep your plan until the end of the time you've paid for. if you cancel during the free two weeks, you pay nothing.

how do i get a refund

refunds are handled by apple or google too. on an iphone, go to reportaproblem.apple.com and sign in with the same apple account. for android, open the play store, tap your profile, then 'payments and subscriptions', pick the order and request a refund. we can't see your card or move money, so we can't do it for you.

what it costs, and how the free weeks work

pick a plan in the app and the first two weeks are free, with nothing charged until they end. after that it's $49.99 a year or $9.99 a month, and apple and google show the price in your currency. the plan renews by itself until you cancel it, and if you cancel before the two weeks are up, you pay nothing.

what happens to my journal if my plan ends

your pages stay exactly where they are, and you can still export or delete everything without a plan. to journal again, pick a plan in the app.

how do i get my journal out

open 'export data' in the 'you' tab. you can download a text file, a pdf laid out to print, or a zip with everything, including your photos and voice notes. it works with or without a plan, even after the free two weeks are over.

how do i delete my account

tap 'delete account' at the bottom of the 'you' tab and confirm. it happens straight away, and your pages, photos, voice notes and banana are all deleted. there's no waiting period and no email to write, so export first if you want a copy.

new phone

install skriblio and sign in the same way as before, with your email, apple or google. there's no password to remember, and your pages, photos, voice notes, memory tree and banana are all there. the lock, and any pages you blurred or hid, only apply to the old phone, so set them up again on the new one.

where my journal is kept

your pages are saved on your phone first, then in a private database in the eu, for as long as you have an account. to see everything skriblio keeps about you, open 'your data' in the 'you' tab.

your memory tree

it's at the top of the 'vault' tab, and it starts as a small sapling that grows as you add favourites. tap the heart next to any page, photo, doodle or voice note and it hangs on the tree. the round button in the corner opens it full screen. swipe to turn it, scroll down to go back through the months, and pinch to zoom in. if you take something out of your favourites, it comes off the tree but stays in your vault.

the transcript is wrong

open the page, tap 'typed up' under the photo, then 'edit', and fix the word. your phone remembers the change for the next page, and the photo stays exactly as you took it. for a voice note, tap the small pencil next to its words.

typing up a voice note

find the page in your vault and tap 'transcribe it'. an iphone does this by itself, without sending the recording anywhere. on android, voice notes stay as recordings for now, because typing them up isn't available there yet.

face id

turn on 'face id lock' in the 'you' tab, and skriblio asks for it whenever you come back to the app. on android it's called 'fingerprint lock' and uses whatever your phone has. it's off until you turn it on, and it covers the app on that phone. while it's on, the 'from your tree' widget shows nothing from your journal.

notifications

skriblio doesn't send any, so there's nothing to switch on or off. if you go quiet, your banana turns up on your home screen widget or your computer instead.

the desktop companion

it comes with both plans, and it needs the phone app. download it onto your computer and start it. then open the 'you' tab on your phone, tap 'desktop companion' and 'make a code', and type that code on the computer. the mac version is an early build you can download now, and windows isn't out yet.

why 18 and over

under south african law a journal counts as personal information, and the law treats everyone under 18 as a child who needs a parent's consent. we'd rather build that properly than badly, so for now it's adults only.

privacy questions and requests under popia or gdpr go to the same address. our information officer is the director of steady labs (pty) ltd. we answer within 30 days, usually much sooner. the privacy notice and terms say the rest.
